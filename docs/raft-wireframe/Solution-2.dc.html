Solving for self-governance

The challenge
  • Many independent organizations would adopt the tool.
  • The client didn’t want a big administrative burden for managing the tool, and they didn’t have any IT support staff to delegate the task to.
  • Adopting organizations didn’t want to depend on the client for changes, or give the client too much control over their internal processes.
  • Re-orgs in the federal space are very common (expected every few years) so anything hard-coded would become obsolete quickly.
Our approach
  1. 1A two-tier structure. Each adopting organization gets a “Community,” run by its own admins, who can create and rearrange their own “Workflow Groups.”
  2. 2Group managers handle membership within a group.
  3. 3Each organization governs itself without the system administrators getting involved.

Solving for visibility

The challenge
  • Information needs to be protected on a need-to-know basis, but this evolves over time.
  • Who needs to see things is more often determined at an organizational rather than individual level. If I send a request to my point of contact at a budget office, that person may review, or it may get passed on to another analyst.
  • Having visibility on the request doesn’t mean I should have visibility on every piece of data related to the request.
  • Moreover, submitters need to maintain visibility on the status of their requests after submission.
Our approach
  1. 1Sending a request to someone in a group allows that user’s peers to view and therefore collaborate.
  2. 2Compartmentalize visibility on the request so I can share information only with people who have the need-to-know or to protect organizational integrity.
  3. 3Request drafters and submitters can maintain visibility on the overall form content and its status (e.g., approved or denied), while reviewers’ comments are only shown to those with a need-to-know, to protect the integrity of the review process.

Solving for permissions

The challenge
  • Because workflows are non-linear and personnel will wear different hats at different parts of the process, permissions cannot be determined ahead of time, once and for all.
  • Moreover, a user’s permissions may only be temporary.
Our approach
  1. 1Coordinator role — who currently holds the request, distinct from the original creator — given full permissions. The form creator is the original Coordinator.
  2. 2Coordinator can send taskers to other users, temporarily giving them additional permissions on the request, for example, make edits or append a review.
  3. 3Transfer tasker changes the Coordinator role.

Solving for reviews and taskers

The challenge
  • Duplicate questions often went back and forth over email, leaving no centralized, easily accessible record of reviews and coordination.
  • No one could see what was still open: questions and rework requests sat in inboxes with no status, owner, or due date, so tracking them meant chasing people.
Our approach
  1. 1Reviews and taskers act as sub-objects of the Funding Request object.
  2. 2The tasker is the single unit of coordination, with four types:InformationReviewReworkTransfer
  3. 3Taskers and resultant reviews create a coordination history that can be viewed.
  4. 4Group and individual dashboards allow for tracking of workloads.
